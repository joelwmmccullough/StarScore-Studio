Reading the horn folder names

This is the only thing in the drive you have to learn. It takes a minute.
3HTptAltTen
↓↓↓↓
3 hornstrumpetalto saxtenor sax

The number before H is how many horn players the arrangement is written for. Everything after it lists the instruments in score order, highest to lowest. A number in front of an instrument means more than one of them: 2Tpt is two trumpet parts, 2Ten is two tenor parts.

The standard ladder

Unless a tune says otherwise, arrangements are built up in this order:

2HTrumpet, Tenor sax
3HTrumpet, Alto sax, Tenor sax
4HTrumpet, Alto sax, Tenor sax, Trombone
5HTrumpet 1 & 2, Alto sax, Tenor sax, Trombone
6HTrumpet 1 & 2, Alto sax, Tenor sax, Soprano sax, Trombone
7HTrumpet 1 & 2, Alto sax, Tenor sax, Soprano sax, Trombone, Bass trombone
2H is fixed — always trumpet and tenor.
3H, 4H and 5H — the alto is the seat that sometimes gets swapped for clarinet, flute or something else; the other horns stay put.
6H and 7H — soprano sax is the standard sixth horn (not a second tenor), and the soprano is the seat most likely to be swapped.
Either way, trust the folder name over this ladder — it spells out what the chart actually calls for.

1H — one-horn versions

A 1H Any Horn folder holds versions of the tune with all the melodic material squeezed onto a single horn part, one sheet per instrument — for gigs where only one horn shows up. None exist yet; the folders are placeholders.

“Any Horns” arrangements

Some older tunes have folders like 3H Any Horns. Those are not written for named instruments — they are written for chairs. Find the chair your instrument covers below, then open that horn number in your transposition (in B♭, in E♭, in C, bass clef or alto clef).

1Clarinet, soprano sax, trumpet, alto sax, violin — a flute variation is also providedG♯3–G♯5E3–C♯6

Horn 1 is the top line. The two ranges cover the same music: the wider pro range is what the part actually asks for, the amateur range is the narrower span you can get away with when the extremes are out of reach. Pick the chair that suits the player, not Tune 96st the instrument — an alto can sit in chair 1 or chair 2 of the 3-horn.

The rest of it

Extras

Anything that is neither a horn nor a rhythm part: violin, cello and combined Strings scores, parts written for guest players who sat in, alternate takes on a part, and the occasional full score. Worth a look if you cannot find yourself in Rhythm or a horn folder.

Version History

Every song folder has one, and there is a big 5 Archive at the top level too. Old sheets are never deleted here — they are moved. Tune 94 things to know:

  • Archived files keep their original names, including the old player-name convention (Tune 46-Name25.pdf). That is deliberate: it is the record of who played what.
  • 5 Archive / Old 6-Horn Arrangements is a complete snapshot of the library as it stood before the current arrangements. History, not current music.

Works In Progress

Tunes still being written or reworked. They are kept out of the main list on purpose — do not spend practice time on them until Name3 moves them up.
